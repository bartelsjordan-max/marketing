Prepared forDigital Engagement Leadership

SubjectOperating model, not technology
The shift

For decades, marketing has focused on building campaigns.
The future of marketing focuses on building relationships.

Today, marketers ask

"What email should we send next week?"

Tomorrow, marketers ask

"What does this member need right now?"

That single question changes everything downstream of it — how content gets built, who owns what, how success gets measured, and what Enterprise Marketing exists to do.

Setting expectations

This is not a presentation about Adobe, AI, or personalization technology.

It's a presentation about transforming Enterprise Marketing from an organization that manually builds campaigns into one that designs an intelligent member decisioning system.

Emails are no longer manually designed
Campaigns no longer dictate every communication
Merchandising still owns products
Enterprise Marketing owns orchestration
AI assists decisioning — it doesn't replace strategy
The member relationship becomes the organizing principle
Framework

Five levels of marketing maturity

A real sequence — each level is a precondition for the next.

01CampaignEveryone receives the same communication.
02SegmentDifferent audiences receive different campaigns.
03BehaviorCommunications respond to purchases and browsing.
04DecisionEvery communication is assembled based on the member's context.
05RelationshipEvery communication strengthens the long-term member relationship.

Costco's email program today operates primarily at Levels 1–2, with pockets of Level 3. This briefing is the case for Levels 4 and 5.

Current state

Today's organization is built around campaign production.

The question we ask

"What email are we sending?" — a production question, asked and answered fresh every week, regardless of who's on the other end.

Why this breaks

Three members. One email.

A young family

Needs Back to School basics, kids' clothing, snacks — receives the general merchandise email anyway.

A college student

Needs a laptop, dorm furniture, a mini fridge — receives the same general merchandise email.

An Executive member

Needs travel, Executive benefits, electronics — receives the same general merchandise email.

The campaign is optimized.
Every product, every offer, every send date — planned.

The member experience is not.
Campaigns assume every member wants the same thing at the same time. That assumption becomes increasingly incorrect.

The batch-and-blast reality

The process today

Job posted to the eCom graphics board — about a week before deployment
↓
First proof due the next day; buying is included in the first two proofs
↓
Coordinator builds the entire layout by hand — one row per product, in a shared spreadsheet
↓
Every description manually reformatted — dashes to "with," ampersands to "and," shipping checked line by line
↓
Two buyer proofs plus one peer review, tracked by manually highlighting spreadsheet cells green
↓
Subject lines and headers drafted, emailed to an approval group for sign-off
↓
6am day-of audit, then send
The critical path

Roughly five business days — for one email, to one list.

Reading the same process differently

What this actually costs

One send, one audience — no branch point anywhere for who's receiving this
One technical bottleneck — total volume capped by one developer's queue, not by demand
No reusable content — every module rebuilt or copy-pasted fresh, every cycle
Approval by spreadsheet color, not by system — governance that can't scale
A ~5-day critical path makes reactive, behavioral marketing structurally impossible

Attendees: eCom Merch, Travel, Gas, Foods, Non-Foods, Pharmacy, Services.
Input: each vertical brings its priorities for the coming month — Travel's booking-window pushes, Pharmacy's refill and enrollment timing, Gas and Foods' seasonal needs, merch's category pushes, membership's renewal cohorts.

Two systems, not one

Two audience layers

Eligibility — FAC
  • Who's allowed to see it
  • Rule-based, deterministic
  • Membership tier, tenure, region, opt-ins
  • Slow-changing, governed, auditable
Relevance — AJO Decisioning
  • Who should see it
  • Scored, probabilistic
  • Propensity models, recent behavior, lifecycle stage
  • Continuously refreshed

A module only gets considered once it clears eligibility. It only wins a slot based on relevance.

Merchandise becomes one possible experience — not every experience. Silence is a valid, deliberate outcome of the model.

Decisioning in practice

This is already underway

Operation: Snowplow — the 90-day new member acquisition and onboarding journey currently in build — applies this exact waterfall logic in AJO today.

Proof point, not the focus

Snowplow prioritizes new-member onboarding needs ahead of general merchandise offers, sequenced against real AJO rule sets, frequency caps, and a standing 10% holdout for measurement. It's the first live example of Level 4 decisioning inside Costco's current stack — not a separate initiative from what this briefing describes.

Conclusion

Enterprise Marketing no longer functions primarily as a campaign execution team.

It becomes the operating system that determines:

Who receives communications
When they receive them
Why they receive them
What experience they receive
How success is measured

The email itself becomes the output of an intelligent decisioning system — not the product marketing manually creates.
